## 0. Summary

**What the feedback tells us (evidence).** One real user dogfooding both halves of the app on a phone hit **three
bugs that lose his work or look broken**: the recipe photo upload fails and shows `[object Object]`; a set's kg and
reps values render in near-microscopic type; and a custom "Indoor bike" gets coached as `3 × 1 s @ 15 kg`. He also
hit **one structural gap**: cardio has no shape in the data model, and the library has no cardio exercises. Everything
else is friction in flows we already planned to touch: recipe-form effort, logger layout, the Resume card, and workout
history.

**What it changes (opinion).**

1. **This feedback mostly confirms the plan.** 12 of the first 21 items (and the planned parts of O-22 and O-23 in the addendum) are duplicates or extensions of Now items that are
   already scheduled in W1/W2 (B-05, B-36, B-21, CI-38's T-01.6). The synthetic panel found the same pain first. Real-user
   corroboration raises **confidence** on CI-42 (set delete), CI-52 (history), CI-20 (endurance/cardio), CI-02 (the
   "36-box" recipe form) and CI-38 (recipe edit).
2. **Three gaps are genuinely new:** cardio as a first-class exercise type (**B-42**), a governed, curated exercise
   library (**B-43**), and correcting a past workout (**B-44**). There are also two food items: a recipe form you can finish on
   mobile (**B-40**), and a curated generic-ingredient catalogue as a data foundation (**B-41**).
3. **A parity gap surfaced, and it's a process bug.** The web recipe form already has an ingredient search
   (`IngredientPicker`), a canonical unit list (`ingredients.units`), cuisine presets, auto-computed macros
   (`ingredients.computeNutrition`) and private custom ingredients (`ingredients.createCustom`). The mobile form has
   none of these: it uses free text everywhere, typed macros, `fiber: 0` hard-coded, and six required fields. No
   `mobile_parity_backlog.md` row covers it. Most of what the owner asked for on ingredients **already exists on web**.
4. **Two owner asks collide with standing strategy. Both are resolvable** (§2): the ingredient database vs B-29, and
   restricting custom exercises vs "editable at every level".
5. **Sequencing matters more than scope.** Custom exercises must not be restricted **before** a cardio catalogue exists.
   Otherwise the only way to log a bike ride today disappears, and the owner's own cardio bug becomes impossible to work
   around.

**Placement at a glance:** a small **W0-D hotfix PR** now (photo upload, tiny values) → **W1** amendments in
L-GYM and L-SAFE (logger layout, set delete, timed defaults, Resume card, recent workouts, swap sheet, editor card, two new
exercises, the image audit, recipe-form slice 1) →
**W2** L-GYM (edit past workouts; optionally minimal cardio, D-20) and a new lane L-RECIPE (ingredient search on
mobile) → a new **W5 "Data foundations"** (curated ingredient catalogue, full cardio, exercise-library governance).

## 2. Conflicts with existing strategy, and synergies

### 2.1 An ingredient database vs B-29 "Don't race MyFitnessPal on a branded-food database or barcode"

**The apparent conflict.** B-29 (re-examined twice and kept) says not to build a food database. The owner asks for
"a pretty strong data set" of ingredients.

**Why it isn't the same thing (opinion, with evidence).** B-29 rejects the **branded, barcode-scannable, crowdsourced**
database: millions of SKUs, where MyFitnessPal, Cal AI and Eat & Track own the moat [S3][S35], and where data quality
decays because anyone can add a product. The owner asks for something structurally different, and **most of it already
exists** (O-20):

| Dimension      | B-29's "Don't" (branded DB)            | What the owner asks for (B-41)                                                                 |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Unit of data   | A product SKU ("Danone Activia 125 g") | A **generic ingredient** ("oats, rolled, dry"; "chicken breast, raw")                          |
| Size           | 10⁶–10⁷ rows                           | ~800–1,500 rows cover a home cook (opinion, to size in W5)                                     |
| Who writes it  | Crowd, then moderation                 | **Admins** (curated), seeded from a licensed reference table                                   |
| User additions | Public, shared                         | **Private to the user** (`creatorId`, as today), never shared, so there's no moderation burden |
| Barcode        | Core                                   | **None**                                                                                       |
| Job served     | "Log my packaged snack"                | "Build and cost my recipe; know what's in it"; the tracker's gram search is a by-product       |

**Recommendation.** Keep B-29 exactly as it is: **no brands, no barcodes, no public user entries**. Add the boundary
explicitly to B-29's card in the Rev 4 note. Build B-41 as a **curated generic-ingredient catalogue** with
user-private custom rows. It is a data foundation, not a tracking-depth play, and it answers CI-28 the way B-19 already
intended ("search over Chefer's own ingredient catalogue in grams"). **Guardrail:** if the owner later asks for
"add Lidl's skyr" as a shared entry, that is B-29 territory and stays Don't.

**Synergies (why B-41 is worth more than the owner's ask).**

- **Safety (B-01, B-02):** today the matcher reads free-text ingredient lines against pattern lists, and misses things
  (granola → tree nuts, spelt/seitan/malt → gluten: bugs B-02 and B-47). With lines linked to a catalogue ID that
  carries **EU Annex II allergen tags** (the 14 [S61]) and diet flags, safety becomes a lookup for linked lines. Free
  text keeps the pattern matcher as a fallback. B-01's "diet tags derived, not hard-coded" gets a reliable input,
  and B-02 can cite the ingredient ("Checked for: tree nuts, incl. granola"). **Caveat:** "may contain" and
  brand-dependent risk (stock, oats) stay a label caveat (B-02). A generic row can't know the brand.
- **Numbers you can trust (B-11):** recipe macros computed from ingredient lines make every recipe number tappable to its
  sum ("626 kcal = 80 g oats 303 + 250 g skyr 158 + …"). That is P10's "show your work", applied to own recipes.
- **Food logging (B-19, CI-28 Sev 4):** T-19.1 already searches the catalogue per 100 g. Verified values and private
  custom ingredients reachable from the Log sheet raise its quality without a branded DB.
- **Money and lists (B-14, B-16):** `IngredientPrice` already holds per-100 g prices. Canonical units cut "3.1 ml
  cinnamon" (bug B-52) and let the list merge lines.
- **Import (B-17):** imported lines can be mapped to catalogue IDs in the review draft (D7), so imports gain safety and
  macros for free.

**Risk.** Licensing (D-16). AI-estimated rows that look authoritative would make B-11 worse, not better. So B-41 marks each
row's `source` and "verified" state, and the UI says "estimated" where it is.

### 2.2 Restricting custom exercises vs "editable at every level" ⚖

**Standing decision (evidence).** The gym workstream memory and `gym_plan.md` D5: "Everything is editable at three levels:
(a) the routine, (b) this week and today's session, (c) the engine's targets." The library scope (`gym_plan.md` Exercises
tab) includes "Create custom exercise … no media". All gym features are free.

**What the owner's new ask actually changes (opinion).** Less than it seems. D5's three levels are **routine, session
and targets**. They are untouched by restricting library authoring. Users still edit their routines, swap exercises,
add sets and override targets. What changes is one feature: **authoring new library entries** moves from every user to
admins, or to a trainer role. I record it as the owner's **updated decision** (O-08). It amends the "custom exercises
allowed" part of the gym decisions, not D5 itself.

**The conflict that remains.**

1. **Sequencing risk.** Today a custom exercise is the **only** way to log anything the library lacks: all cardio,
   loaded bodyweight (P01's "Bodyweight + load", CI-42), niche machines. Removing it before the library covers those
   cases strands users. **Recommendation:** B-43's restriction ships **after** B-42's cardio catalogue and a
   library-gap pass. In the meantime, hide `+ Custom` behind a flag (off by default for new users, on for users
   who already have custom exercises) only once coverage exists.
2. **Expert control (B-05's "protect D1–D4").** Lifters with unusual programmes value control. **Recommendation:** replace
   `+ Custom` with **`Request an exercise`** (name, equipment, optional link). It lands in an admin queue (web admin
   page), and the user gets a temporary private placeholder that is **log-only** (no engine suggestions, so no
   O-02-style nonsense). When an admin promotes or maps the request, the placeholder's history moves to the curated
   exercise. This keeps "I can always log what I did" while the library stays clean.
3. **The gym-free decision (D-11).** A "trainer" role must not become a paywall. It's an **authoring permission**,
   not a tier. (A future coach marketplace is a separate Later idea, not part of this.)
4. **Backward compatibility (CLAUDE.md).** Shipped binaries call `gym.library.createCustom`. Enforcing a role
   server-side makes old clients show a raw error. **Recommendation:** first hide the button over OTA with a flag, then
   enforce on the server with a clear `FORBIDDEN` message for level-0 clients after the OTA adoption window. This is the
   same pattern as `HEALTH_CONSENT_ENFORCE`.
5. **Architecture (CLAUDE.md rule 5):** gating belongs in middleware (a `trainerProcedure` next to `adminProcedure`),
   not in an `if` in the handler. Tech note for stage 4: curated exercises are synced **from code** at boot
   (`exercise-catalog.ts`, `ensureExerciseLibrary`). Trainer-authored rows need their own provenance so the boot sync
   never overwrites them.

### 2.3 Removing fiber vs tracking and targets items

**Evidence.** No Now or Next item sets or shows a fiber target: B-35 is kcal + protein with optional carbs/fat; B-11
explains targets; B-20 covers goal weight and trend. The one place fiber comes up is CI-44: the prediabetic persona (P06)
noticed "no carb/sugar/fibre framing". That segment is served through B-22 and B-30 (wellness, not medical), and B-30
forbids diabetes-management claims.

**Recommendation.** Take fiber out of **input** (the web form's `Fiber (g)` box) and out of **default display** (the web
recipe stat), matching mobile and the owner's "3 macros". **Keep the data**: `nutritionInfo.fiber` stays optional in
the API (old clients send `fiber: 0`, and removing the field would break them) and `IngredientPrice.fiberPer100g`
stays, because B-41 will compute it for free from ingredients. If the health segment is pushed later, fiber can come
back as a computed, read-only line with no input box. That call is D-18.

### 2.4 Other tensions worth naming

- **"Mandatory fields" vs safety (B-01).** Making ingredients optional would let a recipe exist with no lines to check.
  **Recommendation:** name and ≥ 1 ingredient stay required; description, cuisine, steps and times become optional
  (D-19). A recipe with no steps simply has no cook mode.
- **Editing past workouts (B-44) vs "never change a number silently" (B-11).** Editing a past set recomputes
  progression, which moves next week's target. **Recommendation:** after an edit that changes a suggestion, show the
  B-11 change notice ("Next time for Bench Press: 62.5 kg → 60 kg, because you edited Tuesday's sets").
- **Cardio calories vs B-06 and B-22.** A MET estimate carries ±15–25 % individual error (06 §3). Feeding it into food
  targets without a cap or a visible note repeats the silent-target-change problem that CI-06 punished. See D-17.
- **Cardio vs the D-12 "no health sync" decision.** B-42 is manual-first. `avgHeartRateBpm` is only an additive slot
  (06 §5.2). Nothing here reopens D-12.
