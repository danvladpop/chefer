# Chefer Persona Study — the 10 personas

Audience per owner: solo cooks, households and gym-goers. The mix covers gym-only, food-only and
both; beginners to experts; RO / UK / US / DE locales (metric vs imperial, RON / GBP / USD / EUR);
iOS + Android; small and large screens; dark mode and large text; free-staying and
premium-inclined users.

## P01 — Andrei, 24, bulking gym bro (Cluj-Napoca, Romania)

- **Life:** junior developer; trains 5×/week on a push/pull/legs split; 3 years of lifting. 78 kg,
  wants 85 kg lean. Eats chicken, rice and eggs on repeat; shops at Lidl/Carrefour; budget-minded (RON).
- **Tech:** power user, Android. Uses Strong (lifting), MyFitnessPal (on and off), and YouTube for form.
- **Traits:** impatient with onboarding, skips text, taps fast, hates being sold to. Judges an app in 2 minutes.
- **Why he downloaded it:** a friend said it does "workouts + meal plans in one app, free".
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up fast; 2) set up his PPL (or find one), with his real working weights
  (bench 90 kg ×6, squat 120 ×5, deadlift 150 ×5); 3) log today's push workout, set by set,
  including an extra set and a swapped exercise; 4) see what he should eat to hit ~180 g protein
  on a budget.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) start the next workout from the notification or home screen fast; 2) see if the app suggests heavier weights; 3) log a protein shake + a meal quickly; 4) check
  his lift progress / PRs.
- **Sweep list:** exercise library + detail + custom exercise; routine editor (reorder, supersets);
  gym stats; gym settings (units, rest timer, reminders); offline logging (turn wifi off in the
  emulator's quick settings, log a set, turn it on); Food/Gym switch discoverability.

## P02 — Maria, 30, trains 3×/week and meal-preps on Sundays (Bucharest, Romania)

- **Life:** marketing manager, long days. Lifts Mon/Wed/Fri (upper/lower), wants to recompose
  (lose a bit of fat, get stronger). Meal-preps lunches on Sunday for Mon–Thu. 63 kg, 168 cm.
- **Tech:** iPhone 17 Pro Max in **dark mode** (set `appearance dark` on L2 at the start).
  Pays for 2–3 apps already (Strava, a period tracker, Spotify). Used MyFitnessPal Premium and
  cancelled it: "too much logging".
- **Traits:** efficient, aesthetic-sensitive, will pay for things that _save time_. Notices
  polish and inconsistency.
- **Why she downloaded it:** an Instagram ad, "your week of meals + workouts, planned".
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up and set her goal (recomp), training days, dislikes (no mushrooms); 2) get a week of meals that fits a Sunday meal-prep (same lunch Mon–Thu); 3) get the shopping list
  for Sunday; 4) set up an upper/lower gym routine for Mon/Wed/Fri. Decide whether premium is worth it.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) Wednesday: log her upper-body workout; 2) swap a dinner she doesn't
  fancy; 3) check that training days eat a bit more (does the app know she trained?); 4) look at her week at a glance.
- **Sweep list:** premium upgrade flow + post-upgrade sheet; downgrade; My weeks / templates;
  meal swap vs replace; plan carry-forward; weekly review (coach); dark mode consistency on every
  screen visited; notifications permission + settings.

## P03 — Jake, 19, total beginner (Columbus, Ohio, USA)

- **Life:** college sophomore, dorm with a shared kitchen, $60/week food budget. Has never
  followed a workout plan and can cook pasta and eggs. Wants to "get in shape" and stop living on
  instant noodles. 5'10", 150 lb. Thinks in **pounds, ounces, cups, °F and USD**.
- **Tech:** iPhone 16e, TikTok native, zero patience for forms. Doesn't know what "macros",
  "RIR", "hypertrophy" or "PPL" mean.
- **Traits:** easily overwhelmed, gives up silently, needs to be told exactly what to do.
  Reads a headline, not a paragraph.
- **Why he downloaded it:** searched "workout and meal plan app free" on the App Store.
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up; 2) get a simple beginner workout plan he can do at the campus gym; 3) understand how to do an exercise he doesn't know (e.g. Romanian deadlift); 4) find 3 cheap,
  easy meals he can actually cook this week.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) do his first workout following the app (log it, even if he isn't sure what
  weight to use); 2) cook one recipe step by step; 3) figure out what to buy with $60.
- **Sweep list:** units and currency (imperial/USD everywhere?); jargon audit (every term a
  beginner wouldn't know); empty states; help/tooltips; cook mode; exercise technique media;
  onboarding copy; the free-tier limits (what he hits and how it's explained).

## P04 — Elena, 38, feeds a family of four (Brașov, Romania)

- **Life:** part-time accountant; partner Mihai (40); kids Ana (9) and Luca (6). **Luca has a
  tree-nut allergy** (EpiPen-level serious). Ana is a picky eater (no fish, no "green stuff").
  Wants healthier weeknight dinners in ≤ 40 minutes, one weekly shop, less arguing at the table.
- **Tech:** iPhone 17, uses WhatsApp, Glovo, a shared Google Keep shopping list with Mihai.
  Moderately tech-comfortable.
- **Traits:** safety-first on the allergy (will check every recipe), time-poor, practical, would pay
  if it genuinely removes the "what's for dinner" stress for the whole family.
- **Why she downloaded it:** a parents' WhatsApp group recommendation.
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up; set up the household (4 people, 2 kids) and the **tree-nut
  allergy** + Ana's dislikes; 2) get a week of family dinners; 3) verify that **no recipe contains
  nuts**, and see how the app reassures her; 4) get one shopping list for the week, scaled to 4 people.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) Mihai asks "what's for dinner tomorrow?" (find it fast); 2) replace a
  dinner Ana won't eat; 3) tick off the shopping list in the store (one-handed, quick); 4) cook tonight's dinner using cook mode.
- **Sweep list:** household flow (premium gate?); allergen handling everywhere (recipe detail,
  swaps, imports, chat); shopping list sharing with a partner; pantry; portion scaling; ratings;
  premium upgrade reasoning.

## P05 — Priya, 34, vegetarian with 15-minute weeknights (London, UK)

- **Life:** software engineer, lives with her partner (who also eats vegetarian), orders
  takeaway 3–4× a week and feels guilty about it. Lacto-vegetarian (eats dairy, no eggs). Wants to
  cook 4 nights a week, quick. Metric, **GBP**.
- **Tech:** iPhone 16e, heavy Notion and Instagram-saves user (has 200 saved recipe reels she never cooks).
- **Traits:** analytical and curious, likes customising, skeptical of "health" apps that feel
  like diet culture.
- **Why she downloaded it:** wants to finally _cook_ the recipes she saves.
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up as vegetarian (no eggs); 2) import 2 recipes she saved (one
  website link, one pasted text; try an Instagram/YouTube link too); 3) build a week with 4 quick
  dinners; 4) see if every suggestion is really vegetarian and egg-free.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) find a ≤ 15-minute dinner for tonight from what's in her fridge; 2) favourite / organise recipes; 3) edit an imported recipe (fix an ingredient); 4) make it for 2.
- **Sweep list:** recipe import (URL / text / video link / photo), cheferize, the recipe form + photo
  upload; cookbook search/filter/sort; favourites; recipe detail readability; AI chat (premium gate
  for free users; is it clear?); pantry "cook from what I have".

## P06 — Tom, 52, prediabetic and told to lose 10 kg (Manchester, UK)

- **Life:** warehouse operations manager; his GP said HbA1c is borderline and to lose ~10 kg
  (currently 102 kg, 180 cm). Wife cooks most meals but he wants to "do his part". Walks the dog;
  the GP suggested strength training twice a week, and he has never been in a gym.
- **Tech:** iPhone 17 Pro Max with **large text** (set `content_size extra-extra-large` on L2 at
  the start; revert to `large` at the end). Reading glasses. Uses WhatsApp, BBC News, Facebook.
  Low tech confidence; afraid of "pressing the wrong thing".
- **Traits:** careful, reads everything, needs reassurance, dislikes jargon and tiny text;
  trusts numbers if they are explained. Would pay if his GP would approve.
- **Why he downloaded it:** his daughter installed it for him.
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up (slowly, reading everything; he mistypes his password once); 2) tell the app about his blood sugar concern and his weight-loss goal; 3) understand what
  he should eat today and why; 4) find a gentle 2-day beginner strength routine.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) log his weight; 2) log what he actually ate at lunch (a meal that
  wasn't on the plan); 3) see whether he's "on track"; 4) find out how to change something he
  set up wrong yesterday.
- **Sweep list:** accessibility at large text (truncation, overlaps, tap targets); forgot-password
  flow; weight tracking + progress charts; tracker quick-add; medical/diabetes handling (does the
  app say anything responsible?); settings discoverability; account deletion flow (find it, don't
  complete it); feedback flow.

## P07 — Ioana, 27, cutting and logging every bite (Iași, Romania)

- **Life:** PhD student and amateur powerlifter, cutting from 66 to 61 kg for a competition in
  10 weeks. Weighs her food. Needs 2,000 kcal / 150 g protein. Eats out with friends twice a week.
- **Tech:** Android power user in **dark mode** (`adb -s emulator-5554 shell cmd uimode night yes`
  at start, `night no` at the end). Has used Cronometer and MacroFactor for years; very
  opinionated about logging speed and data accuracy.
- **Traits:** precise, fast, notices wrong numbers immediately; will pay for accuracy and speed.
- **Why she downloaded it:** curious whether snap-to-log could replace weighing restaurant meals.
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up with exact targets (2,000 kcal, 150 g P); 2) log breakfast,
  lunch and a snack as fast as possible; 3) photograph a restaurant meal (snap-to-log) and
  judge the flow; 4) see remaining macros for the day.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) correct a mis-logged entry (edit/delete); 2) log a meal eaten out; 3) weigh-in + trend; 4) check the week's adherence.
- **Sweep list:** tracker (quick add, edit, delete, units, precision); snap-to-log (camera vs
  photos, permission denied path); the plan's macros vs her custom targets; progress & weight;
  week rebalance; coach review; premium mechanics; dark-mode consistency.

## P08 — Daniela, 45, cooks for two and hates waste (Timișoara, Romania)

- **Life:** high-school teacher, empty nester with her husband. Big weekly shop at Carrefour, and a
  lot gets thrown away. Collects recipes from YouTube/Facebook videos. Budget-aware (RON).
- **Tech:** iPhone 17, Facebook and YouTube, comfortable but not techy. Types slowly.
- **Traits:** thrifty, organised, loves a list, suspicious of anything that wants her card.
- **Why she downloaded it:** saw "zero-waste pantry" in the app description.
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up; 2) enter what's in her fridge and pantry (6–8 items); 3) get meals that use them before they go off; 4) import a recipe from a YouTube cooking
  video link; 5) see what the week's shop will cost.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) mark items used / add new groceries after shopping; 2) see what's
  about to expire; 3) cook something from the pantry; 4) share the list with her husband.
- **Sweep list:** pantry end to end; shopping list prices/currency; video-link import + review form;
  meal plan from pantry; carry-forward/leftovers; household (2 people) gating; notifications.

## P09 — Chris, 36, getting back to the gym after a new baby (Leeds, UK)

- **Life:** product designer; a 5-month-old at home; hasn't trained consistently for 3 years
  (was intermediate: bench 80 kg once). Gets 2–3 sessions a week of 30–40 minutes, at
  unpredictable times. Sleep-deprived. Wants to "not fall off again".
- **Tech:** iPhone 16e, uses Apple Health, Headspace (lapsed), Strong (lapsed).
- **Traits:** a designer, so he notices UX; forgiving but easily discouraged by guilt-trippy
  streaks; values flexibility and quick wins.
- **Why he downloaded it:** wants something that adapts when he misses sessions.
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up; 2) set up a short full-body routine, 2–3×/week; 3) do and log a 30-min session, cutting it short halfway (baby wakes up); 4) see how the app treats an unfinished session.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) he missed his planned day. What does the app do or say? 2) pause training for a week (a family trip) and resume; 3) quick healthy dinner idea
  for a tired parent; 4) set a reminder that fits a chaotic schedule.
- **Sweep list:** training pause; missed-day / backfill handling; streak and consistency
  mechanics (tone!); reminders; session summary; history; Apple-Health-style stats;
  Food/Gym switch; cross-links between food and training.

## P10 — Lena, 29, vegan, coeliac, runner who lifts; privacy-minded (Munich, Germany)

- **Life:** UX researcher, runs 40 km/week and lifts 2×/week. **Vegan + coeliac (strict
  gluten-free, cross-contamination matters)**. Needs high carbs on long-run days. EUR, metric.
- **Tech:** iPhone 17 (L3; originally planned on Android), reads privacy policies, declines analytics, GDPR-aware; has used
  Cronometer and Yazio.
- **Traits:** skeptical of AI nutrition claims ("show me where the number comes from"),
  will pay for trustworthy and safe results; zero tolerance for an unsafe suggestion.
- **Why she downloaded it:** looking for vegan + gluten-free meal planning that knows about training.
- **Session 1 goals:** 1) sign up; handle every consent/tracking prompt deliberately (decline
  analytics); 2) set vegan + gluten-free (coeliac) and her training; 3) get a week of meals and
  audit them for gluten/animal products; 4) ask the AI chat something (premium?) and judge the trust cues.
- **Session 2 goals:** 1) swap a meal and check the swap stays vegan + GF; 2) find where the
  app says what happens to her data / AI provider; 3) revoke AI consent; 4) export/delete data?
- **Sweep list:** consent flows (AI data consent, analytics consent), privacy/AI provider disclosure,
  account deletion (walk through, **cancel at the final confirmation**), dietary safety in every AI
  surface (plan, swap, chat, import), premium perks list honesty, 2 gym sessions/week setup.
