# P03 — Jake, 19, total-beginner college sophomore on a $60/week food budget (Columbus, OH)

L1 / iPhone 16e (iOS 26) · tier at start Free → end Premium (upgraded in S2, to unlock the weekly budget) · build f8f7f74 · 2026-09-26

## 0. Expectations before opening

- Jake typed "workout and meal plan app free" into the App Store and picked this one because the icon looked clean and it said free.
- He expects: tap a couple of buttons, get told "do these exercises on Monday" and "eat this", with no homework.
- He has no idea what macros, RIR or PPL mean and won't look them up; if the app talks like a bodybuilding forum he feels dumb.
- He'll bail in the first 5 minutes if: sign-up is a long form, it asks for a credit card or throws a paywall, or it shows him a wall of numbers without saying what to do next.
- Win condition: a plan he can open at the campus gym tomorrow and 3 meals he can cook in a dorm kitchen for cheap.

## 6. Researcher summary (out of character)

- **The gym flow is the product's best beginner experience.** Starting-guess weights, the honest "Why?" sheet, the plain-language effort question and "Next time" targets with one-line reasons removed Jake's biggest fear ("what weight do I use?"). Protect this; it is the reason he'd come back (tomorrow-score 7).
- **Budget is the core food job for this segment, and the app fails it on free and does not visibly deliver it on premium.** No budget question in onboarding, budget field premium-gated (and silently not saved on free), no per-recipe price, no cost filter, and after setting $60 the plan read ≈ $69.79 with no over-budget signal (mock AI — verify with real AI; the missing warning is real).
- **Locale leaks break trust for a US user:** RON prices by default, onboarding asks lb/kg but not currency, oven temps stay °C, some ingredients stay in grams, Preferences body metrics in cm/kg while Imperial is selected, 24-hour reminder picker. Each one is small; together they make the app feel "not for me".
- **Beginner-hostile jargon clusters in onboarding and Stats** ("3 × 8-12", delts, 1.5 sets, Relative strength, Flex week, unlabeled stacked chart). There is no glossary or first-time explanation of sets × reps.
- **Food and gym are well cross-linked after the fact** (post-workout protein nudge naming his next meal; Food Today showing today's workout) — but onboarding is single-choice, so a "both" user never gets food setup, and cold start lands on Food even for a gym-first user.
- **Beginner cooking needs more than terse steps:** cook mode is clean, but steps lack times, doneness cues and pictures, and quantities have false precision (0.2 oz chives). Recipe catalogue search needs synonyms (pasta ↔ spaghetti) and a skill/equipment dimension for dorm cooks.
- **Discoverability papercuts:** exercise names are not tappable in the onboarding preview and open a target editor in Routine; set deletion is long-press/"…" only; "Regenerate this week" CTA doesn't regenerate; "THIS WEEK ⌄" hides Regenerate.
- **Post-workout state is confusing:** right after finishing A, Today offers B with a big "Start workout" the same day, and "0-week streak" sits next to "1 of 3 this week".
- **Upgrade mechanics are clean and honest** (one tap, "free for now", three next steps), but the trigger was a locked control on his core need, not perceived value — and the premium payoff (budget-true plan) did not visibly materialise.
