# Chefer — Expert UX/Product Review (Beta)

**Date:** 2026-08-23 · **Reviewer persona:** senior UI/UX designer, 20+ yrs, arriving cold at `https://chefer.duckdns.org/` knowing only "it's a food tool that helps you eat cleaner."
**Method:** live production walkthrough. Fresh signup (`ux-review@chefer.dev`, throwaway), full free-tier tour, one-click beta upgrade to premium, AI plan generation + recipe import + chat (4 of the daily AI budget), household + preferences setup, downgrade back to free, logout. Desktop 1280px and mobile 375px. All findings observed first-hand; screenshots referenced by page.

---

## 1. TL;DR verdict

**The product inside the app is far better than the product the internet sees.** Once signed in, Chefer is charming, cohesive, and genuinely differentiated (per-week cost estimates, cheferized recipe import, honest beta pricing FAQ). Time-to-value is outstanding: a brand-new user has a full 7-day plan on screen in under a minute.

The two things standing between this and a credible launch:

1. **Trust in the numbers.** The premium AI plan I generated missed the calorie target it had just computed for me by 25–45% on every single day. In a nutrition product, the numbers _are_ the product. This is the #1 issue in this review.
2. **The front door.** The logged-out landing/auth experience looks like a different (and much more generic) product than the app, tells almost none of the story, and gives a cold visitor no reason to believe.

No redesign needed — the in-app design system (editorial serif, cream/brown warmth) is a keeper. The work is: fix data trust, re-skin the front door to match the app, and tune the premium funnel details listed below.

---

## 2. First impressions — landing & auth (logged out)

What a cold visitor gets: hero ("Your personal chef, powered by AI"), three feature cards (weekly plans, personalized goals, smart shopping lists), a copyright line. That's the entire page.

**Findings**

- **L-1 · Brand split.** Landing and auth pages are generic SaaS blue/indigo with system sans; the app inside is warm cream, brown, and an editorial serif. It literally feels like signing up for one product and being teleported into another. The app's identity is the better one — re-skin landing + login + register to match it. This is the highest-leverage visual fix in the whole product.
- **L-2 · The landing undersells by an order of magnitude.** Five premium features shipped (adaptive coaching, photo logging, recipe import, household plans, pantry planning) and none are mentioned. No product screenshot, no example plan, no social proof, no pricing signal, no FAQ, no "free during beta" hook — which is currently your single best acquisition line. A cold visitor has no reason to believe the "in seconds" claim; show a real plan grid.
- **L-3 · Credibility gap.** `duckdns.org` domain + a footer with nothing but a © line (no about/contact/imprint links on the landing itself; Terms/Privacy only appear on the register page). For a product asking for body weight and health goals, this reads risky. Even a beta needs: a real domain, a one-line "who makes this," and a contact/feedback link.
- **L-4 · Auth pages are fine functionally.** Clean forms, show-password toggle, required markers, terms links, "Keep me signed in." No email verification in beta (acceptable now; will need it before real launch, along with a verified password-reset flow — the "Forgot password?" link exists but I didn't exercise it).
- **L-5 · Console hygiene.** The register page logs a minified React #418 hydration error (captured by Sentry) plus two 404'd resources. Known pattern in this codebase (pages under a route-level `loading.tsx`); worth sweeping auth pages with the same `useHasMounted` fix.

## 3. Onboarding — good bones, one bait-and-switch

Signup drops straight into a 2-step wizard. Step 1 (diet type, allergies as chips, dislike chips, all optional, "skip anything") is exactly right — low pressure, high signal.

**Findings**

- **O-1 · Step 2 of 2 is not onboarding — it's an upsell.** The progress bar says "Step 1 of 2 · 50% complete," and step 2 is titled "You're all set" followed by a premium pitch card ("Go further with a personal profile") with an Upgrade CTA. As a new user I felt tricked: I gave you my allergies, you gave me an ad. Recommendation: make step 2 _actual_ profile collection (goal + body metrics — free users can store them too, even if only premium plans consume them), and move the pitch to the finish screen as a secondary card. Bonus: this fixes O-2.
- **O-2 · The first plan is built on a default nobody chose.** Because onboarding never asks for goal/body metrics, the first plan targets a generic 2,000 kcal and the dashboard ring says "of 2,000 kcal" — invented numbers presented with confidence. Collecting metrics in onboarding (optional, skippable) would make even the free curated plan feel personal, and would make the premium "built around your body" pitch demonstrable instead of hypothetical.
- **O-3 · Copy nit.** The dashboard greets a brand-new account with "WELCOME BACK, CHEF." First visit should say "Welcome, chef."

**What's great:** signup → seeing a full, appetizing 7-day plan takes under 60 seconds (free plans draw from the curated pool, so there's no AI wait). This instant-gratification loop is the app's superpower — protect it.

## 4. The free tier — a real product, not a demo

Toured: dashboard, meal planner, recipe detail, cook mode, shopping list, tracker (+ scan gate), pantry, progress, chat.

**What works well**

- **Planner grid** is rich and scannable: per-meal macros, time, kcal, day totals, and the **≈€/week cost pill** — a genuine differentiator no mainstream competitor surfaces. Keep leaning into cost.
- **Free-plan banner** ("chef-picked recipes that respect your allergies and restrictions. Upgrade for AI plans tailored to your goals and taste") is honest and well-worded — it frames free as curated, not crippled.
- **Recipe detail** is complete (servings scaler, per-serving nutrition, rating) and **cook mode** (huge step text, progress bar, ingredient checklist) is kitchen-ready.
- **Ghost previews** for locked features are a best-practice pattern and well executed: the scan modal shows a fake scan result with confidence label, the pantry page explains the premium mechanic before asking for money.
- **Empty states** consistently teach + point somewhere (recipes collection, pantry → "Open Shopping List", progress → tracker link).

**Findings**

- **F-1 · Free plans undershoot the (default) target too.** Day totals ran 1,345–1,690 kcal against the stated 2,000 kcal target, while the ring chip said **"ON TRACK."** See P-1 below — same root issue as premium, and "on track" against −500 kcal of plan is not on track.
- **F-2 · Swap is instant with no undo.** Tapping "Swap Recipe" replaced my breakfast in ~1s with no confirmation or undo toast. Fine for exploration, but a mis-tap silently discards a meal the user may have wanted. Add a 5-second "Swapped — Undo" toast.
- **F-3 · Shopping list scale.** One week = 97 items. Grouping and est. prices are great; the wall is still intimidating. Quick wins: collapse categories by default with counts, a "staples you probably have" divider (pantry mechanic already exists — tease it here for free users), and a per-category progress bar for in-store use.
- **F-4 · Chat answers about food you haven't eaten.** With zero meals logged, "How much protein am I eating today?" → "You're eating 92g of protein today." It's reading the _plan_, not the log, and states it as fact. For a coach persona, precision of claim is everything: "Your plan for today adds up to 92g; you haven't logged anything yet." (Also: 92g didn't match the planned-day macro panel — see P-1.)
- **F-5 · Minor UI inconsistencies.** Two planner cards render titles in orange with no legend (appear to be swap-suggestion hints — if a card is special, label it); the Pantry page header reads "Chefer" while every other page shows its name; browser tab titles are inconsistent (`Preferences | Chefer` vs bare `Chefer` on dashboard/planner/recipes); a few shopping-list items lack thumbnails while neighbors have them.

## 5. Premium — the pitch is good; the funnel has gaps; one promise breaks

### 5.1 How premium is advertised (assessment: strong, slightly naggy)

The `/premium` page is genuinely well-crafted:

- Benefit-led headline ("A chef that knows you — and your week") and card names that sell outcomes ("Your week, ready every Monday", "One plan that feeds the whole table") rather than features.
- Free-vs-premium table with real quotas (3/20 plans/day, 5 chat msgs/day, 30 swaps/day…).
- **Price anchoring** ("What this replaces": ~€166/yr of separate apps → "Free during the beta") — excellent.
- **"Fair questions" FAQ** is the most trust-building thing in the app: free now, no payment details _ever_ asked in beta, nothing deleted on downgrade, advance notice before pricing. Every beta product should crib this.

Gaps:

- **P-4 · The comparison table's FREE column opens with three "—" dashes** — including for "AI meal plans tailored to you," where free users actually get a curated weekly plan. First impression of the table is "free gets nothing." Use labels, not dashes: "Chef-curated" / "Curated pool" in the FREE column where a real (lesser) equivalent exists.
- **P-5 · The upgrade dialog is a 10-item checklist wall.** All ten features, equal weight, small text. Show the top 3 benefits (ideally source-aware — you already pass the source) + "and 7 more →". Ten checkmarks read as marketing; three read as reasons.
- **P-6 · Upsell surface count.** Persistent sidebar card + planner banner + recipe-page hint + pantry banner + scan modal + onboarding step 2 + chat-quota touchpoint. Each is individually well-behaved (and the nudge-cap helper exists), but the _cumulative_ impression by minute five is "everything nags." Since premium is currently free, consider converting the permanent sidebar card into a one-time dismissible "Premium is free during beta — try the AI chef" banner; it converts better than wallpaper and buys goodwill.
- **P-7 · No price anywhere.** Deliberate (recorded product-owner call) and defensible in beta; the FAQ's "you'll hear well in advance" is the right mitigation. Flagging only that "free during beta" + no anchor price trains users to value premium at €0 — the "what this replaces" section is currently your only counterweight. Keep it prominent.

### 5.2 The upgrade moment

One click, instant, no payment details — exactly as promised. **But nothing happens next.** The modal closes, the sidebar chip changes to "Premium plan," and… that's it.

- **P-8 · No post-upgrade activation step.** The single highest-value next action — _set your goal and body metrics so the AI has something to personalize against_ — is never suggested. I had to find Preferences myself. Ship a "You're premium — 3 things to do first" moment (set targets → regenerate week → try import). Without it, a fresh premium user regenerates on defaults and premium looks identical to free.

### 5.3 The premium promise vs. what it delivers

I set: Lose Weight, male, 38, 178cm, 86kg, moderately active, Italian+Mediterranean, 3 meals/day, €60/week budget. Computed target: **2,271 kcal/day**. Then regenerated.

The good: ~35s generation with a pleasant "Crafting your week…" state; the week came back visibly Mediterranean/Italian (minestrone, caprese, cod with olives), respected the peanut allergy and onion dislike as far as I could see, and priced at **€42.93 — under budget**, later re-rendered as €21.46/person when I added a household member. AI food photos filled in progressively and look surprisingly good.

- **P-1 · CRITICAL — every generated day missed the calorie target.** Day totals: 1,290 / 1,574 / 1,700 / 1,360 / 1,300 / 1,270 / 1,335 kcal against a 2,271 target — 25–45% under, every day. The target already includes the −500 deficit, so a compliant user would run a 1,400–1,500 kcal _actual_ deficit — unhealthy territory presented under an "ON TRACK" badge. This inverts the core paid promise ("every plan is built around them"). Fix server-side, not with copy: validate generated day totals against the target (e.g., ±10%), retry/expand portions on miss, and if you must ship a miss, badge it honestly ("This day is 900 kcal under your target"). _This is the one finding I'd block a paid launch on._
- **P-2 · The dashboard "ON TRACK" chip and coach copy amplify P-1.** The status chip evaluates "planned ≤ target" so an undershooting plan always looks virtuous. It should flag both directions.
- **P-3 · The Preferences calorie preview shows maintenance, not your target.** Under "Used to calculate your personalised daily calorie target" it displayed **2,771** (pure Mifflin-St Jeor TDEE) while my actual target — shown everywhere else — is 2,271. Two different "targets" one screen apart erodes exactly the trust premium is selling. Show the goal-adjusted number, with maintenance as secondary context.

### 5.4 The rest of premium, quickly

- **Import (Cheferize) is the standout feature.** Link/paste/photo tabs; side-by-side Original vs "Cheferized for you"; explicit change log ("Removed onions due to user dislike", rescaled to 1 serving); saved with the source photo; source URL retained. This is demo material — lead marketing with it. One bug: the honesty banner said "the page says 495 kcal/serving, our ingredient data computes **~125 kcal**" — the ~125 figure is obviously broken math for a pasta dish; when the computed estimate is implausible (>3× divergence), suppress the number and just say the estimate is uncertain, or you look _less_ credible for showing your work.
- **Household** is clean: portion presets (½ kid → 1½ big eater), kid flag, per-member diet/allergy rules, "cooking for 2" chip, per-person cost. Gap: nothing tells you the _existing_ plan won't rescale until you regenerate — a one-line hint after adding a member would prevent confusion.
- **Downgrade** matches the FAQ promise: inline "Lose AI plans, swaps and auto-generation? Yes, downgrade / Keep premium," instant, and the AI-generated week + imported recipe survive on the free tier. Genuinely trust-building.
- **Photo counter is buggy.** The planner chip went "1 of 17 photos ready" → "0 of 11" → "1 of 11" → "0 of 8" across a few minutes — the denominator appears to be the _remaining_ queue while the numerator resets. Cosmetic, but it's on the flagship screen; make it monotonic ("14 of 17") or just show a subtle spinner + "photos generating."

### 5.5 Preferences page (the premium control panel)

- **PR-1 · Save is all-or-nothing.** Footer: "Fill in your goal, biological sex, age, height, weight, and activity level to save." A user who only wants to add a cuisine or set a budget can't save without completing a full body profile. Split the page into independently-saved sections (diet, household, goals/metrics, cadence, budget, units) or autosave per control.
- **PR-2 · It's one very long page** doing seven jobs; fine on desktop, a scroll marathon on mobile. Section tabs or sticky in-page nav would help.
- **PR-3 · Accessibility.** The goal cards and sex selector expose no accessible names to the a11y tree (unnamed buttons); activity radios announce raw enum values ("SEDENTARY", "MODERATELY_ACTIVE"). Also **PR-4:** the keyboard focus ring on goal cards is visually identical to the selected state — tabbing across cards makes two look chosen. (I genuinely couldn't tell which goal was active until I inspected `aria-pressed` — a sighted-user problem too.)

## 6. Mobile (375px)

Solid. Bottom tab bar (Home/Plan/Recipes/Shop/More), day-at-a-time planner with photo thumbnails, calorie ring dashboard, cost pill including per-person split. The existing automated mobile sweep (overflow + touch targets at 320–430px) is clearly paying off — nothing structural to report from this pass.

- **M-1 ·** "SUSTAINABLE CHOICE" badge on the dashboard header has no explanation anywhere — tapping does nothing. Either make it a tappable "why" or cut it; unexplained badges read as decoration.
- **M-2 ·** The chat FAB sits directly over the last meal card's macros at the bottom of the planner list (above the tab bar). Consider hiding it on scroll-down or nudging list bottom-padding.

## 8. Does it need a redesign?

**No.** The in-app system — editorial serif headers, cream surfaces, brown/amber accents, generous cards — is distinctive, appetizing, and consistently applied across ~12 screens. Two exceptions to converge:

1. Landing + auth (L-1) — re-skin to the app's identity.
2. Stray blue elements inside the app: onboarding Continue/Finish buttons, pantry "Open Shopping List" button, dislike-chip selection state, weekly-budget & household modal controls. All should use the brand accent. (The blue reads as leftover default-theme.)

---

## 9. Banners & promos worth adding (in-app)

1. **"Premium is free during beta" one-time banner** (dashboard, dismissible) — your strongest current offer, currently only discoverable via upsell surfaces that look like ads. Honest version converts better: "Everything's free while we're in beta — help us test the AI chef."
2. **Post-first-plan personalization prompt** (free users): "This week is chef-picked. Tell us your goal and we'll shape it around you →" — bridges free value to premium value in one tap.
3. **Monday moment** (premium): "Your week is ready" — the auto-generated-Sunday feature is a retention gem; announce it when it fires or nobody will know it happened.
4. **Sunday-review teaser** (free + premium): the "log 3 more days and your chef writes you a Sunday review" dashboard line is great — echo it in the tracker where logging actually happens.
5. **Feedback ask** after high-signal moments (first plan, first import): "Was this week right for you?" — doubles as the quality telemetry P-1 needs.

## 10. Prioritized recommendations

**P0 — trust (before promoting AI features or any paid launch)**

1. Validate/retry AI plan generation against the calorie target (P-1); fix "ON TRACK" logic (P-2).
2. One canonical calorie target everywhere (P-3).
3. Chat claim precision — planned vs logged (F-4).
4. Feedback channel in-app (§7.6).

**P1 — funnel & front door** 5. Re-skin landing/auth to app brand + tell the real feature story + beta-free hook (L-1/L-2/L-3). 6. Onboarding step 2 = goals/metrics for everyone; move upsell to a card (O-1/O-2). 7. Post-upgrade "3 things to do first" activation (P-8). 8. Preferences: sectioned saves (PR-1); focus-vs-selected styling (PR-4). 9. Upgrade dialog: top-3 benefits, not ten (P-5); FREE column labels, not dashes (P-4).

**P2 — polish** 10. Swap undo toast (F-2); photo-counter semantics (§5.4); Profile page: move Gemini/Pollinations telemetry behind an admin flag — end users should never see your vendor quotas (it reads hobby-project and the numbers contradict product quotas); shopping-list collapsing (F-3); a11y names on goal/sex/activity controls (PR-3); title-bar and page-title consistency, orange-card legend, "Welcome back" copy, SUSTAINABLE CHOICE badge (F-5, M-1, O-3); register-page hydration error + 404s (L-5).
